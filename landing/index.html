It never invents a number for you.

No marks entered? oHub says unknown and asks — where other tools hand you a confident percentage built on nothing. Every figure traces to something you entered or a university published, and links out to the official page.

Your marks stay yours.

oHub asks for your name, school, OUAC reference and grades — the kind of thing you would not post publicly. Signing in keeps it across devices, so it is worth being exact about where it goes and who can reach it.

  • Your data stays in Canada. Stored in a Canadian region, not shipped somewhere cheaper.
  • Only you can read your row. Access is enforced by the database itself, keyed to your account — not by app code that could have a bug.
  • No tracking, no analytics, no ads. Nobody is counting your clicks or selling your data, and it is never used to train anything.
  • Your OUAC reference is masked everywhere except the field where you edit it, and it is never written to a log.
  • Delete it and it is gone. One control in Settings, and anything left is purged automatically 18 months after your cycle ends.
  • Google tells us your name and email. Nothing else — oHub cannot read your Gmail, Drive or calendar. The whole notice is one page.
