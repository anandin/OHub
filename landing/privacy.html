What oHub stores, and why

oHub is used by Grade 12 students, most of whom are under 18. This page is the whole of it — not a summary of a longer document kept somewhere else.

Last updated 16 August 2026.

What we hold

Only what you type in, plus what Google tells us when you sign in.

What Google gives us

Your name, your email address, and a Google account identifier. oHub requests no other scopes: it cannot read your Gmail, Drive, Photos or Calendar, and could not be granted that access without asking you again on Google's own consent screen.

Where it lives

In a Postgres database hosted by Supabase in Canada (ca-central-1, Montréal), and in your browser's local storage on each device you sign in on, so the app keeps working when the network does not.

Every table has row-level security switched on, with policies that match rows to your account id. There is no "read everything" role in the application: the key shipped in the app has had its table access revoked outright, and a query can only return rows your own signed-in session is entitled to. A table added without a policy returns nothing rather than everything.

Who can see it

  • You, on any device you sign in on.
  • Nobody else. Not other students, not your school, not your teachers. oHub has no counsellor accounts, no class rosters, and no admin view of student data.
  • Supabase, as the hosting provider, in the sense that any database host can technically reach the disk it runs on.

oHub does not sell data, run advertising, or load third-party analytics. There are no tracking pixels and no advertising identifiers. The site's content security policy blocks scripts from any origin but our own, so one could not be added quietly.

How long we keep it

Your records carry a deletion date eighteen months out, refreshed while you are using the app. Nobody needs a 2026 applicant's marks in 2030.

Deleting it

Settings → Delete my account removes your account and everything attached to it, immediately. No email, no support ticket, no waiting period. Signing out separately clears the copy held on that device.

Deletion is permanent. There is no backup we can restore your list from afterwards, which is the point.

Under 18

oHub is built for Grade 12 students and expects most of its users to be 17. It asks for the minimum that makes the tool work, stores it in Canada, and puts the delete control in the app rather than behind a request process — because a consent you cannot withdraw yourself is not much of a consent.

If you are a parent or guardian and want a student's data removed, the student can do it in Settings in about five seconds. If they cannot, write to the address below.

Changes

If what we store or where it lives changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. It is version-controlled alongside the app's source, so the history is public.
